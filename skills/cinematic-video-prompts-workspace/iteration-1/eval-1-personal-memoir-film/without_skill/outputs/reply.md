Your worry is the right worry — that's the number one failure mode. But the fix mostly isn't better prompt wording. It's four decisions you make before you generate anything.

**1. Pick a look where mismatch reads as texture.** Go "recovered 16mm, 1956" — grainy, desaturated, overcast, handheld, slightly damaged. Under that treatment, two clips that don't quite match read as two different rolls of film from the same box, which is what real archival footage actually looks like. Clean modern photorealism is the mode where every mismatch screams.

**2. Don't show her face.** Faces are where consistency breaks worst, and the room will be full of people who know her actual face — a synthetic near-miss of a beloved 90-year-old, at her party, in front of her, lands badly. So: **AI supplies the world, real photographs supply the person.** Shoot her from behind, at distance, in silhouette, hands only. Then cut to a real scanned photo of her at twenty for the payoff. That contrast is a better film than anything fully synthetic.

**3. Make an object the character.** Since the face can't carry continuity, hand the job to wardrobe: "a brown wool coat with the top button missing, dark green headscarf, small dark leather case." Repeat that string word for word in every prompt. Viewers track the coat, and the coat becomes her.

**4. Paste an identical style block into every prompt — literally copy-pasted, not paraphrased.** Paraphrasing is how drift happens. And lock five things absolutely: one aspect ratio, one clip length, overcast winter always, one palette, and zero camera movement. That last one matters more than people expect — one drone shot among seven handheld shots does more damage than any color mismatch.

Two more things worth knowing:

**The edit does more work than the prompting.** One color grade and one grain overlay across the whole timeline in DaVinci Resolve (free) will unify clips that look mismatched as raw exports. Almost every guide skips this step and it's the highest-leverage thing you'll do. Same for sound: a single unbroken audio bed running across every cut makes the audience read discontinuous images as one continuous memory.

**Go record her telling the story, this week.** Her own voice over these images is worth more than every prompt I've written, and it's the one asset you can't generate later. Even if it doesn't make the final cut, get it on a phone voice memo.
